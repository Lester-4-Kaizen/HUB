Ethics and Responsible Data Use

📎Module 01 — Introduction to Advanced Data Analytics · Giralyn R. Ongco, PhDTM
Advanced data analytics influences critical decisions in business, government, healthcare, and education. Because of this power, ethical and responsible data use is essential. The four pillars are: Data Privacy, Data Accuracy, Bias in Analysis, and Responsible Interpretation of Results. Unethical data use can harm individuals, create discrimination, damage institutional reputation, and lead to legal consequences.
⚠️

Why Ethics Matters in Data Analytics

Advanced data analytics influences critical real-world outcomes:
  • Business decisions
  • Government policies
  • Healthcare outcomes
  • Educational opportunities
  • Loan approvals & hiring decisions
  • Medical treatment & security systems
Unethical use of data can: harm individuals, create discrimination, damage institutional reputation, and lead to legal consequences. Therefore, ethical and responsible data use is essential.
🔒

1. Data Privacy

Pillar 1
Data Privacy
Definition: The protection of individuals' personal and sensitive information from unauthorized access, misuse, or exposure.
Why It Matters:
  • Protects individual rights
  • Prevents identity theft and fraud
  • Builds trust between organizations and stakeholders
  • Complies with data protection laws
✅

2. Data Accuracy

Pillar 2
Data Accuracy
Definition: Ensuring that information used in analysis is correct, complete, and reliable.
Why It Matters:
  • Inaccurate data leads to wrong conclusions
  • Poor decisions can harm organizations and individuals
  • Damages credibility and trust
⚖️

3. Bias in Analysis

Pillar 3
Bias in Analysis
Definition: Bias occurs when data or analytical methods unfairly favor certain outcomes, groups, or perspectives.
Why It Matters:
  • Can lead to discrimination
  • Produces unfair or unethical decisions
  • Undermines trust in analytics systems
🎯

4. Responsible Interpretation of Results

Pillar 4
Responsible Interpretation
Definition: Explaining results accurately without exaggeration, distortion, or misuse.
Common Risks:
  • Misleading visualizations
  • Overstating predictive certainty
  • Ignoring limitations
  • Confusing correlation with causation
👤

The Ethical Data Professional

A responsible data professional must:
  • Protect privacy
  • Ensure accuracy
  • Avoid bias
  • Communicate honestly
  • Respect legal and ethical standards
🔍 Correlation vs. Causation — A Key Misinterpretation Risk Interpretation ›
Correlation: Two variables change together (e.g., ice cream sales and drowning incidents both increase in summer). Causation: One variable directly causes the other (e.g., swimming more → more drowning risk). Confusing correlation with causation is one of the most common and dangerous analytical errors. Just because two variables move together doesn't mean one causes the other — there may be a third confounding variable (summer heat, in this case).
💡 Always ask: Could this correlation be explained by a third variable? Does the causal mechanism make logical sense?
📊 Types of Bias in Data Analytics Bias Types ›
Selection Bias: The sample data doesn't represent the full population (e.g., using only urban hospital records to study national health trends). Confirmation Bias: Interpreting data to support pre-existing beliefs while ignoring contradictory evidence. Algorithmic Bias: ML models trained on biased historical data reproduce and amplify those biases (e.g., a hiring algorithm that learns from past decisions made by biased managers).
💡 Bias often enters through the data collection phase — which is why representative, diverse datasets and rigorous validation are critical ethical safeguards.
🌍 Real-World Consequences of Unethical Data Use Impact ›
Real-world examples of unethical data use: Biased hiring algorithms — Amazon's experimental AI hiring tool was found to penalize resumes that included the word "women's" (e.g., women's chess club). Discriminatory lending — algorithmic credit scoring that disproportionately denied loans to minority applicants. Misused health data — sharing patient data with third parties without consent. These cases resulted in legal action, regulatory investigations, and reputational damage.
💡 Ethical data use is not optional — it is increasingly regulated through laws like GDPR (Europe), PDPA (Philippines), and CCPA (California).
